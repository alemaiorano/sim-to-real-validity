\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:correction}
LLM-based audience simulation offers an inexpensive way to compare messages before live testing.
Demographic conditioning can approximate aspects of survey samples~\citep{argyleSiliconSampling},
but a copy-ranking system must predict measured engagement. We study this narrower question with
Upworthy headline experiments~\citep{matiasUpworthy}.

\paragraph{Revision and evidence status.}
An external artifact review prompted a full consistency audit of release \texttt{7c1ab46}.
The tables reproduce stored reports, but that agreement does not validate the estimators.
The original rank implementation computed Goodman--Kruskal gamma while the paper called it
Kendall tau. The winner screen selected the observed best arm before a one-sided test.
Undefined coefficients were excluded without effective denominators. Claims about baseline
superiority everywhere, prompt robustness, and a causal explanation also exceeded the artifacts.

This revision distinguishes historical tables, retaining original numbers with corrected labels,
from an offline historical GPT-4.1 diagnostic and a new matched-budget experiment. The diagnostic
reuses recovered checkpoint scores after replay of the pinned original producer reproduces the
published report; it cannot undo historical response parsing or unequal budgets. The new experiment
collects both model families on the same corrected Upworthy cohort, with raw responses preserved
and matched score counts within each model. It addresses those collection limitations for new
observations, without retroactively validating historical generations or other datasets.

We ask (RQ1) how this panel and generic baseline compare within tested packages, (RQ2) how
agreement varies with the observed outcome signal, and (RQ3) what the dataset, aggregation,
seed, and model comparisons establish. This evaluates a specified harness rather than all
persona methods or audience representativeness.
