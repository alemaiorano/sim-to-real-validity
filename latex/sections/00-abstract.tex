\begin{abstract}
Do demographic personas improve an LLM's prediction of audience engagement? We compare a fixed
ten-persona panel with a generic-reader baseline on Upworthy headlines, with supplementary
news and Reddit comparisons. The primary experiment collects Gemini 3.1 Flash-Lite and GPT-4.1
observations on the same \newSelectedN{}-package exploratory cohort, selected with simultaneous
winner screening. It allocates thirty scores per arm and variant within model, retains exact raw
responses, and reports metric-specific valid and paired counts. Complete-package coverage is
\newGeminiN{} for Gemini and \newGptN{} for GPT-4.1. The baseline-minus-panel Kendall $\tau_b$ gap is
\newGeminiGapTau{} (CI [\newGeminiGapTauLow{}, \newGeminiGapTauHigh{}]) for Gemini and
\newGptGapTau{} (CI [\newGptGapTauLow{}, \newGptGapTauHigh{}]) for GPT-4.1, on respectively
\newGeminiPairedTauN{} and \newGptPairedTauN{} valid rank pairs. Expected top-1 is
\newGeminiPanelTopOne{} versus \newGeminiBaselineTopOne{} for Gemini and \newGptPanelTopOne{}
versus \newGptBaselineTopOne{} for GPT-4.1 (panel versus baseline). These intervals condition on
the selected cohort and recorded generations and do not adjust for dependence between related experiments. The Upworthy archive documents a randomization-risk
period. Excluding that period in a post-hoc sensitivity retains positive expected top-1 and lift
gaps in both configurations, while Gemini tau-b and Spearman intervals include zero. Supplementary
Reddit top-1 and captured lift favor the panel; these aggregate comparisons use unreconciled
collection protocols and do not isolate dataset effects. A raw-replay artifact supports offline reproduction of the primary experiment.
The results concern this panel, cohort, and protocol; they are not a held-out evaluation or
evidence of universal persona harm. Provider request protocols differ across models, and no
causal mechanism is isolated.
\end{abstract}
