\begin{abstract}
Do demographic personas improve an LLM's prediction of audience engagement? We compare a fixed
ten-persona panel with a generic-reader baseline on Upworthy headlines, with additional news and
Reddit comparisons. This revision corrects metric identity, selection, ties, and claims in the
original release. Historical Upworthy estimates favor the baseline: Goodman--Kruskal
$\gamma=\baseGamma{}$ versus $\goodmanGamma{}$, and top-1 \baseTopOne{} versus \topOneAcc{}.
These used a post-selection one-sided winner screen and first-index tie breaking; they remain
historical results, not corrected Kendall estimates. The baseline does not dominate every dataset:
Reddit top-1 and captured lift favor the panel. A retrospective GPT-4.1 diagnostic uses a two-sided,
multiplicity-adjusted winner screen, explicit metric denominators, and expected credit for tied
choices. On \correctedPairedN{} paired packages, its baseline advantage persists. This diagnostic
reuses recorded post-parser values, retains unequal sampling budgets, and cannot validate original
responses. Historical Gemini observations were unavailable for equivalent reanalysis. The evidence
supports a baseline comparison for the tested configurations, not a general causal claim about
persona harm. Public reports support numerical verification; complete historical reproduction
from raw observations remains incomplete.
\end{abstract}
